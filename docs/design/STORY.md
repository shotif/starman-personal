# The story: a proposal

*Written 24 October 2026 for the owner's decisions D-36 to D-41. It fills in D-07 (the stars are
going out, with a villain who has a face) on the Starman canon (research/STARMAN_UNIVERSE.md).
Everything here is fiction, labelled so in the game; the stars and planets named are real. Nothing
is written in ink until the owner answers.*

## 1. The premise in a paragraph

It is the 2180s. Humanity has met the Duskfolk and the Drifters; the Absent's relics are being dug
up at the white dwarfs; the second tone still sounds from Pyre's direction. Then Ross 128, a quiet
old red dwarf 11 light-years from the Sun, starts to dim. Not flicker: dim, a few percent a month,
as Antares did before it went dark. Something is wrapping the star in a swarm of machines and taking
its light. The hero is there when the outpost on Ross 128 b goes dark, comes away with a damaged
ship and a crew that looks to them, and learns that Ross 128 is not the first star, and will not be
the last. Then the hero learns who is helping it.

## 3. The face of the enemy (D-37)

**Recommended: Lightkeeper Ansa Varro**, once the Sol Transit Authority's youngest Lightkeeper,
its highest rank (Reborn's), the officer who kept the lanes lit through the Lapse. She found the
first Absent engine twenty years ago and has studied the dying stars since: Betelgeuse, Antares,
Pyre. Her argument: the stars are dying anyway; the Quench keeps their light safe, banked for after;
helping it is the only mercy left. She leads **the Banked**, people who believe her, from every
power and from both alien peoples. She is right about some things, wrong about the one that matters:
the stars were not dying until the engines came.

Her title is the game's: the hero becomes the new **Lightkeeper**, the one who keeps the stars lit,
in Act 2, when the Authority (or whoever the hero has won) gives it to them.

## 6. The ship (D-40)

The ship the hero takes at Ross 128: a Halden Orbital Works long-haul surveyor (Reborn's maker),
armed after the fact. Her name: **A. *Lantern* (recommended)**, B. *Vigil*, C. *Kindling*.

## 7. Side stories: the rule and three examples

**The rule** (D-04): every side story has people with reasons, a choice that costs something, and a
consequence the hero sees later. If it can be summed up as "go there, bring that back", it is not
finished. Three examples:

- **The last harvest at Harrow Farmstead** (HD 219134): the farm's star is not dimming, but a
  family is selling its grain to the Banked, who pay well. The neighbours want them stopped. The
  hero learns why they sell (a child the Banked doctors saved) and decides what is owed to whom. The
  grain, or its absence, feeds a refugee station in Act 3.
- **The keeper of the Barnard relay**: an old Authority officer who served under Varro, keeping a
  lane beacon lit as traffic dies. His logs are the best record of who she was. He will hand them
  over if the hero tells him the truth about her, which breaks him, or the hero can lie.
- **Salt's line**: descendants of Reborn's Wake captain Salt run a den that takes in people fleeing
  dimming stars, for a price. A smuggling job turns out to be a family the den is selling to the
  Banked as engine crews. Free them, buy them, or bring the den to the hero's side for Act 3.
