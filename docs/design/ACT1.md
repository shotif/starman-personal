# Act 1, first part: *Lantern* and the first lead — a proposal

The prologue is built and played through ([SLICE.md](SLICE.md)). D-20 says what comes next: Act 1,
built in parts. Act 1 is about eight hours ([STORY.md](STORY.md) §4): three leads in any order, each
a place, a power and a companion, each ending in the same name, Varro. That is three times the
prologue, so this proposes its first part: the things every lead needs, and one lead built whole.
The decisions it needs are round 7 in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) (D-47 to D-51).

All of it is fiction but the stars and their planets, as everywhere in the game.

## Goals

1. **The ship becomes home** (D-18). After the prologue the player lives on *Lantern*: walks her
   decks, talks to the crew and the survivors of Ross 128 between missions, and chooses where to go.
2. **A star map of the real neighbourhood.** Real stars at their real places within about 25
   light-years; the three leads on it; travel as a short scene, the crew flying.
3. **The hero grows.** Today nothing changes from the first fight to the last. An act of eight
   hours needs growth: stronger powers, new ones, better weapons (D-49, D-50).
4. **The first human enemy: the Banked**, Varro's followers, who fight from cover, mend each other
   and bring the engines' machines with them. Fights then mix people and machines.
5. **One lead built whole**, about two hours with its side story, recruiting the third companion.
6. **Phone and desktop first-class**, as always (D-02): each place loads on its own, so a phone
   holds one place at a time, not the whole act.

## Out of scope for this part

The other two leads (the next parts of Act 1), the companions' personal quests beyond their first
step, ship battles and encounters in flight (D-18 puts them after this), the full melee system
(D-17), romance (D-15), and the mid-story specialisation (D-10, Act 2).

## 1. *Lantern*, between missions

The prologue showed the bridge and the hold. This adds the rest of a long-haul surveyor, small and
lived in:

| Deck | What happens there |
| --- | --- |
| **The bridge** | The star map; Jo flies; Ren on comms. Where missions are chosen |
| **The crew deck** | Cabins: each companion has one, and talks there between missions |
| **The mess and the hold** | The survivors of Ross 128: Dena cooks, Sorrel keeps things ready, Pell listens, Ilya (if alive) reads curves. What they say changes after each mission, so the prologue's choices stay visible |
| **Engineering** | Jo's, and where the ship's upgrades are fitted (D-18) |
| **The armoury** | Weapons and mods (D-50); the hero's growth (D-49) |

Companions get conversations after each mission: what they thought of it (approval, D-12), and the
first step of their personal quest (Ren: who buried her report; Jo: the crew who sold engine parts).

## 2. The star map

A map of the Sun's real neighbourhood (positions from the Gaia catalogue: the Sun, Alpha Centauri,
Barnard's Star, Wolf 359, Lalande 21185, Sirius, Ross 128, Wolf 1061 and the rest within about 25
light-years), with the game's places on it marked as fiction. The three leads are there from the
start; this part opens one (D-48), and the other two say so honestly ("Act 1 continues in a later
update") rather than pretending. Choosing a place plays a short scene of the ship leaving and
arriving; the loading happens behind it.

## 3. How the hero grows (D-49)

Recommended: **experience and ranks.** Missions, side stories and choices give experience (not
kills, so a peaceful choice is never the poorer one). Each level gives points: a power's rank (each
power three ranks, each rank one clear gain: longer, stronger, a second target), a new power from the
class's list at levels 3 and 6, and talents from the hero's origin and moment (D-34, D-35).
Companions level with the hero; their points the player may set or leave to them.

## 4. Weapons (D-50)

Recommended: **four kinds, two carried.** Pistol, rifle, shotgun and marksman rifle, each class
trained in two or three (D-10), the hero carrying two and switching. Better weapons and mods
(a scope, a stabiliser, a heavier round) are found on missions and bought at stations, not dropped
by every enemy. The sidearm the hero already holds becomes the first pistol.

## 5. The Banked, in a fight

Varro's people (D-37), believers with reasons, not cultists. In this part three kinds:

- **Banked trooper**: rifle, armour plate, takes cover, moves between covers when flanked.
- **Banked engineer**: a shield on an ally, a drone of their own, and sets the engines' motes on the
  hero (the Banked control some of the machines: the first sign of how close they are to Varro).
- **Banked warden**: a heavy with a field barrier the hero must break or go round.

They need what the motes did not: enemies that use cover, flank and fall back. The rules go in
[COMBAT.md](COMBAT.md) with tests, as the swarm's did.

## 6. The first lead (D-48): Pyre Remnant — outline

Recommended first: **Pyre Remnant**, the Cooperative's observatory station that watches what is
left of the star Pyre. It follows straight from the prologue (Ilya's readings, his pointer to the
observatory's century of curves), its companion is a Warden (a healer, unlike Ren and Jo), and it is
a station full of people, so it shows the conversation side of the game before the next fights.

Beats (the full script is written in ink, as the prologue's was):

1. **Arrival.** The station answers docking calls late and says little. Its open band has gone
   quiet. The concourse is busy and tense: researchers, traders, refugees from a colony whose star
   has dimmed, and a Banked speaker in the commons, calmly arguing that the light should be "banked"
   while there is still some left.
2. **The archive is shut.** The station's committee says the century of light curves is "under
   audit". Dr Mira Achterberg, Pyre's last observer, has been suspended for refusing to hand over her
   family's records of Pyre's death (her personal quest, D-39).
3. **People with reasons.** An archivist who keeps a copy and is afraid; a dock worker paid to look
   away; the Banked speaker, who believes what he says; a committee member who thinks the Cooperative
   must stay out of it. Talking, looking, and a choice of whom to trust decide how the hero gets in.
4. **The audit.** Inside, a faction persuaded by the Banked is erasing every curve that falls in
   steps (as Ross 128's did) and will hand the rest to Varro's ship when it docks. The hero fights
   through Banked troopers and the motes they bring, to the archive.
5. **The cost.** The wipe and an attack on the observatory dome come at once: save the archive (the
   evidence, and the Cooperative's trust), or the dome's crew under fire (their lives). The other is
   lost, and the act remembers it.
6. **Mira.** She joins, reads what was saved, and finds the steps at Antares, at Pyre and at a star
   not yet on anyone's list. In the oldest audit, twenty years ago, one name signed it: A. Varro.
7. **Side story — the unlisted.** The refugees on the station are not on its manifest; the
   committee wants them gone before the audit is over, and the Banked offer them passage. The hero
   can take them aboard *Lantern* (crowded, and the committee will not forget), argue their case
   (and lose time the archive needed), or let the Banked carry them (they live, as engine crews). Who
   they become returns in Act 2 or 3 (D-41).

## 7. What it costs

About as much again as the prologue in building: the ship's decks, the map, growth and weapons, the
Banked and their fights, the station and its mission, and every line voiced (D-23). Art: Meshy has
2,210 credits left (D-24); the station, the Banked and the weapons are the biggest pieces. The
increments, each playable and deployed (D-01):

1. Places that load on their own, and the ship's decks to walk.
2. The star map and travel.
3. The hero's growth and the weapons.
4. The Banked, their fights and their rules.
5. Pyre Remnant: the station, its people, the mission, the side story, Mira.
6. Voices, music, models, and play-testing on a phone and a desktop.
